Data-Driven Culture: the problem is not technical, it's human

When people keep using Excel after a new dashboard launches, the problem is rarely the chart.

The problem

I have seen organizations launch technically strong BI solutions while managers continued using the spreadsheets they had trusted for years. That behavior is rational: people trust what they understand, control and know how to explain.

My approach

Data literacy changes that equation. Users need to understand where the numbers come from, what a KPI means, how to interpret uncertainty and when the dashboard should influence a decision. Training therefore becomes part of BI delivery, not an optional extra.

What this means in practice

Stakeholder engagement also matters. The people who use the report should participate in defining requirements and validating the result. Adoption starts before the dashboard is published.

A data-driven culture is built through trust, literacy, governance and repeated use. Technology enables the change; people make it real.
